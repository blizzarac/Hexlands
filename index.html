Hexlands

A turn-based hex strategy game inspired by Antiyoy

Conquer a random island against AI opponents with random personalities.

How to play
  • Click one of your provinces (your colour is blue) to select it.
  • Terrain matters: plains earn 1 coin per round, lush meadows earn 2, rocky hills earn nothing — but a tower built on hills defends one level higher. Farms can't be built on hills.
  • Landmarks are worth fighting over: mines pay +3 income to whoever holds them, villages yield +12 plunder to their first captor, and ancient forts defend their area at level 2 while held. Nothing can be built on a landmark.
  • Farms add 4 income per level — 6 on meadows, so a maxed meadow farm earns 20 a round. Farms are captured intact rather than destroyed, and units can stand on them to guard them. Units cost upkeep every round — if a province goes broke, all its units die.
  • Click a unit, then a highlighted tile to move it. Units move up to their level in tiles per turn (peasant 1 … baron 4), through friendly land only; capturing costs the final step. Freshly bought units can be dropped anywhere in the province.
  • Units capture tiles defended at a strictly lower level than their own — equal defence always blocks, even for level 4.
  • Units, towers and capitals defend their tile and all neighbouring tiles.
  • Merge two units (move one onto the other) to make a stronger one.
  • Towers boost friendly units standing next to them by +1 level (gold ring and +1 badge). The boost applies where the unit stands — park a unit beside a tower and it attacks and defends one level stronger. A boosted level 4 is the only thing that can break a level-4 wall.
  • Chopping a tree earns 3 coins. Trees spread and block income, so keep them in check.
  • Upgrades: with the Tower or Farm button armed, click the existing building (gold outline). Towers climb a four-step ladder — Watchtower (def 2) → Fort ⬡30 (def 3) → Castle ⬡45 (def 4, boost reaches 2 tiles) → Citadel ⬡60 (boost reaches 3 tiles). Defence caps at 4, so a tower-boosted baron can always break a siege. Farms go to level 3 (+4 income each, +6 on meadows).
  • Each AI has a playstyle, shown next to its name: ⚔️ Warlord (attacks relentlessly), 🌾 Builder (farms and expands), 🛡️ Turtle (fortifies), ⚖️ Balanced.
  • The Sell button lets you sell units, towers and farms for 75% of what they cost — handy for downsizing an army before it bankrupts the province.
  • Doctrines: on rounds 5, 15 and 25 every player adopts a permanent empire edict (cheaper units, stronger towers, richer farms…). Picks are public — shown as icons next to each player's name — and there's no luck involved.
  • Hover any tile for its terrain, defence and contents. Your tiles the enemy could capture next turn are marked with a dashed red outline, and tiles that changed hands during the enemy phase pulse (red if they were yours). A red ! on a capital warns of bankruptcy next round.
  • Your game auto-saves after every action — close the tab and pick it up later with Continue.
  • Duel mode is the competitive format: you against one Balanced AI on a fixed, mirror-symmetric map with zero randomness — identical play always produces an identical game, so openings can be studied like chess.
  • Hexed difficulty plays Hard's perfect execution plus planning: expansion aims at open country, captures target tiles that split your provinces, and it garrisons exactly what you could take. Its realms are also blessed — every AI province earns +2 income. You have been warned.
  • Win by destroying every enemy province or controlling 65% of the island (60% in duel). Duels also end after round 60 — whoever holds more tiles wins; equal is a draw.
